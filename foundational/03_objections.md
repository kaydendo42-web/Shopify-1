# Foundational Doc 3 — Objections & Beliefs

> Every objection she's going to have, ranked by frequency, with the exact way to pre-handle it on the page and in ads.

---

## 1. The Big 7 objections (ranked by frequency)

### Objection 1 — "Does this actually work or is it another gimmick?"
**Belief underneath:** Beauty marketing has lied to her before. Defaults to skeptical.

**How to handle:**
- Lead with clinical references (635nm + 830nm wavelengths — these are real, mention them)
- Show before/afters on **age-matched** faces (45+ women, not 22-year-olds)
- Specific timeframes: "Visible firmness in 28 days. Most users report a difference by week 4."
- 3rd-party validation: "Same wavelengths used in clinical red light therapy beds"
- Avoid the word "miracle"
- Include a "How it works" section with the biology (mitochondrial stimulation, collagen response) in plain English

### Objection 2 — "$200 is a lot — what if it doesn't work for me?"
**Belief underneath:** She's been burned before and won't risk it twice.

**How to handle:**
- **60-day money-back guarantee.** Restate 3+ times on the page.
- "Try it for 8 weeks. If your skin isn't visibly firmer, get every dollar back. We pay return shipping."
- Lock the return language in plain English in the FAQ
- Show the guarantee badge in the hero buy-box, in mid-page, in the footer
- Add Klarna/Afterpay so the upfront isn't a single hit

### Objection 3 — "How is this different from Omnilux / CurrentBody?"
**Belief underneath:** She's done her homework. Knows the names. Wants to know why she'd pick *us*.

**How to handle:**
- Comparison table — head-to-head on the specs that matter:
  - Wavelengths (635 + 830nm)
  - LED count
  - Treatment time
  - Coverage area (full face + neck if possible)
  - Cordless
  - Warranty
  - Designed for **perimenopausal skin specifically** (this is the wedge)
  - Australian-based support
- Don't trash competitors. Position as "the same proven technology, designed specifically for women in this phase, at a price that respects you."
- Honest about not being TGA-medical-device approved — own the consumer-wellness positioning

### Objection 4 — "Is it safe for my skin / eyes?"
**Belief underneath:** Especially perimenopausal skin is more sensitive (rosacea flares, thinner skin). Eye safety is a real concern with bright LEDs.

**How to handle:**
- "Eye-safe design. Closed-eye treatment recommended. No UV. No heat damage."
- FCC / RoHS / CE marks where applicable — show them
- Specific safety claim: "Tested for skin types I–V. Safe with rosacea-prone and sensitive skin."
- Auto-shutoff timer reassurance
- One FAQ specifically about "is it safe with retinol / Botox / HRT" — answer all three

### Objection 5 — "Will I actually use it consistently?"
**Belief underneath:** She has 3 skincare devices in her drawer she doesn't use. Doesn't want to add a 4th paperweight.

**How to handle:**
- Reposition treatment time as **short and ritualistic**: "10 minutes, 3–4 nights a week. No serum needed."
- "Light enough to wear while you make dinner / fold laundry / watch your show"
- Lifestyle imagery: woman in a robe wearing the mask while reading, while in the bath, while on a recliner with wine
- Email flow Day 1 → Day 28 → Day 60 to coach habit formation
- "The 28-Day Glow Challenge" — turn the first month into a guided programme

### Objection 6 — "Will I look ridiculous wearing this?"
**Belief underneath:** She's seen the Insta photos. She doesn't want to feel silly. Also doesn't want her partner to see her in it and laugh.

**How to handle:**
- Style the product photography to feel **chic, not clinical** — robes, candles, low light, soft fabric textures
- Show the product on a real woman, eyes closed, peaceful — not the "screaming LED face" stock shot
- Position it as a **moment of solitude** ("your 10 minutes of nobody-needs-anything time")
- Avoid the alien-mask aesthetic — go for spa-coded

### Objection 7 — "What if my partner thinks I'm being vain?"
**Belief underneath:** Layered guilt around investing in appearance. Especially for women who carry a lot of household labour.

**How to handle:**
- Frame as **wellness ritual** not **vanity device** — "10 minutes of quiet, every other night, just for you"
- Lean into the **sleep / mood / mental health** benefits red light is linked to (no medical claims, but the user-experience framing is fine: "wind-down ritual")
- Customer testimonials that mention "the only time of day my brain switches off" — emotional, not vain
- Pair language with **wellness mainstream**: yoga, meditation, journaling, sauna — equate this with those, not with Botox

- "Founded by 47-year-old [name], a [career] who built this for herself first" — founder story

- Visible support email + AU phone hours

## 4. Belief shifts we need to engineer

We're not selling a mask. We're selling **a worldview shift**. The customer journey is:

```
"I'm losing my skin → "I have to fight ageing"
       ↓                          ↓
"I'm in a new phase" → "I can support my skin through it"
       ↓                          ↓
"This is my new chapter" → "And here's the ritual that anchors it"
```

Every page section either reinforces the **old, exhausting** belief (so she sees herself in it) or **pivots her** to the new, hopeful one.

## 5. The single most powerful objection pre-handle

> *"You've spent more than this on serums that hydrate the top half-millimetre of your skin. This works where ageing actually happens — the deeper layers, where collagen lives."*

That sentence (or a tighter version) becomes a key headline on the page — likely in the "why this, why now" section just under the hero.
