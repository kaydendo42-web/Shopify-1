# Foundational Doc 1 — Customer Avatar

> **Product:** At-home LED red light therapy mask
> **Purple Ocean Angle:** Women navigating perimenopause skin changes (38–55)
> **Markets:** AU (primary), then US / CA / UK

---

## 1. Demographics

- **Gender:** Female
- **Age:** 38–55 (sweet spot 42–50)
- **Household income:** AUD $90k–$200k personal, $150k–$350k household
- **Education:** Bachelor's degree or higher (60%+), at least diploma-level
- **Location:** Urban + outer-urban Australia (Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Perth, Gold Coast, Adelaide, Newcastle, Geelong). Also strong in larger regional centres (Ballarat, Bendigo, Cairns)
- **Marital status:** ~70% partnered (married/de facto), ~20% divorced/separated, 10% single
- **Children:** Most have teens (12–22 yrs) or young adults still at home. Some have first grandkids
- **Occupation:** Senior individual contributor or mid-management. Examples: HR manager, executive assistant, registered nurse, primary school teacher, real estate agent, marketing manager, allied health practitioner, business owner, lawyer, GP, accountant
- **Devices:** iPhone primary. Uses Instagram, Facebook, Pinterest daily. TikTok occasionally (lurker more than poster)
- **Spend on beauty/wellness:** $200–$600/month (skincare, supplements, treatments, gym/Pilates)

## 2. Psychographics

### Identity & values
- Sees herself as "the one who holds it all together" — career + home + ageing parents + kids
- High-functioning, time-poor, never asks for help easily
- Used to being in control — perimenopause is the first time her body has felt "out of her hands"
- Values quality over quantity. Will pay more for less if it actually works
- Quietly competitive — looks at her friends' skin and notices who's "ageing well"
- Late-adopter of trends, early-adopter of wellness — only buys after she's read 3+ reviews

### Aspirations
- Want to age **on her own terms** — not the "no effort" anti-anti-ageing crowd, not the "frozen Botox" extreme
- Want to look **rested** more than **young**
- Want to feel **vital** in her 50s and 60s, not depleted
- Want to model self-care for her daughters without lecturing them
- Want to feel **seen** — not invisible to society as she ages

### Tensions
- Hates the word "anti-ageing" but secretly wants the benefits
- Knows skincare alone isn't enough now — something has shifted hormonally
- Aware of the perimenopause conversation online but feels it's mostly Americans (Mary Claire Haver, Dr Vonda Wright) — wants AU voices and AU products
- Suspicious of cheap dropshipped junk on Instagram but also doesn't want to drop $605 on Omnilux without trying first

## 3. Day in the life (composite)

**5:45am** — wakes before alarm (sleep is broken now, used to sleep 7+ hours through). Hot flush at 3am, took 40 mins to get back down.

**6:00am** — coffee, scrolls Instagram in bed for 10 mins. Sees an ad for an LED mask. Saves it. Doesn't click.

**6:30am** — Pilates reformer class or 30-min walk with podcast (currently listening to *Dr Louise Newson* or *The Shameless Podcast*).

**7:30am** — gets kids organised. Looks in the mirror. Notices the line between her brows is deeper than last month. Foundation creasing in places it didn't before. Skin feels drier despite using the same products.

**8:30am** — work commute or home office start. Back-to-back meetings.

**12:30pm** — lunch at desk. Googles "perimenopause skin sagging" between emails. Saves a Reddit thread.

**3:00pm** — afternoon energy dip. Considers another coffee. Has a sparkling water instead.

**5:30pm** — picks up kid, runs to Coles, dinner prep.

**8:30pm** — sits down for first time. Glass of wine (but worries about it). Watches *MAFS* or *Bridgerton* with partner. Scrolls phone.

**9:00pm** — sees a TikTok of a woman doing her LED mask "everything shower" routine. Comments are full of "where did you get yours?" Saves it.

**9:30pm** — does her skincare routine. Standard 4-step. Looks in the mirror. Thinks: *I look tired even when I'm not.*

**10:30pm** — in bed, scrolling. Sees the LED mask ad again. This time she taps. Reads the reviews. Adds to cart. Doesn't check out — partner is talking to her.

**11:15pm** — lights off. Tosses and turns. Wakes at 2am sweating.

## 5. What she has already tried (and why it failed)

| Tried | Why it disappointed |
|---|---|
| **High-end serums** (Sk-II, La Mer, Estée Lauder) | Hydrates and softens but doesn't address the underlying sag, loss of firmness, dullness |
| **Retinol** | Works on texture but leaves her sensitive perimenopausal skin red and peeling — she gives up |
| **Microneedling at a clinic** | $300/session, 4 sessions, scary needle videos, needs downtime, partner asks "what did you do to your face?" |
| **Injectables (Botox, filler)** | Either tried and felt "frozen" or refuses point-blank because she doesn't want her daughter to see her "doing that" |
| **Drugstore LED wand** | Cheap, felt like a gimmick, used it twice, into a drawer |
| **HRT (oestrogen patches)** | Helped systemically but skin is still showing the cumulative damage |
| **Wellness ingestibles** (collagen, evening primrose, sea moss) | Maybe? Hard to tell. No visible result |

## 6. What she desperately wants

- A daily ritual she can **do at home, on her terms** — no booking, no needles, no judgement
- **Visible results in 4–8 weeks** so she doesn't feel like it's another empty promise
- Something that addresses **firmness + glow + texture** at once, not 6 different products
- A product that respects her as an **adult woman with money** — not patronising "miracle" copy
- Something **safe** — TGA-style language, clinical references, no shady claims
- Something that **fits her life** — slips on while she puts dinner on, or while she watches Netflix
- **Quiet luxury** packaging — not loud Gen Z gimmicks. Spa, not TikTok shop

## 7. Brand voice she'll respond to

- Warm but adult. No "babes", no "boo", no Gen Z slang
- Specific, scientific-feeling — but jargon-light
- Acknowledges perimenopause directly without making it a problem to be fixed
- Australian colloquialisms used sparingly (she'll notice if it's not local)
- Owns the "for women over 40" position without being apologetic
- Tone reference: *Frank Body* meets *Mecca's editorial newsletter* meets *Adore Beauty's professional reviews*
